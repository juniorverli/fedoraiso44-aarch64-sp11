## Linux calibration envelope and policy

This is the implemented Linux format, not a verified Windows on-wire format. `SP11CA01` is exactly 36 bytes, little-endian: magic at 0, flags at 8, channel count at 12, two 8-byte records at 16 and 24 (`u32` R0 Q24, `s16` T0 Q6, `u16` reserved), and CRC32 at 32 over the first 32 bytes using the kernel's `crc32_le(~0, ...)`. The current loader checks size, magic, two channels, CRC, R0 within 80–125% of its supplement nominal and T0 within -10 to 60 C. Those checks validate the implementation's own bounds, not that the values or bounds are physically safe. It copies a 20-byte payload (count plus the two records) to parameter `0x080011f5`; the 108-byte ACDB VI record `0x08001535` is a different object from the 36-byte envelope and must not be treated as its schema. The loader currently accepts unknown flags as non-nominal and discards nonzero reserved words. Candidate 0024 rejects both before DSP send. It also rejects unknown `SP11CL01` flags, whose known bit denotes CPS requirement.

The loader marks a valid file nominal only when bit 0 is set; the guard refuses nominal unless `sp11.allow_nominal=1`, and refuses nominal in degraded diagnostics even when that parameter is set. The loader may still transmit nominal data before the guard reaches its unmute decision. Neither that parameter nor a DSP SET_CFG acknowledgement proves a measured R0/T0, correct DSP schema, sound safety, or protection. The staged file's SHA-256 is `5ec3b83465f9cb8627900d3132f82686db44225712a37415ef6db8eddb36b805`: its length, magic, CRC, known flag, two channels, zero reserved words, assumed 25 C T0 and equality to the supplement's nominal R0 passed offline checks. Do not publish the binary. The Q24/nominal interpretation remains as graded in the previous audit.
