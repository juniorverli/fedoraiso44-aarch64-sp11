Important v7 consequences, Verified from the posted final files:

- OF switches to per-compatible protocol data. Generic G6 uses **0b/02**;
  this unit's captured **eb/e2** must be separate match data. RFC 0013 now
  does that as well as its 300 ms reset / 10 ms shutdown behavior.
- The binding drops the generic `hid-over-spi` fallback and address/opcode
  DT properties. RFC 0015 adds a specific MSHW0484 enum; revised draft 0012
  removes the fallback and those obsolete properties.
- Error-counter sysfs attributes are dropped. The test plan uses kernel
  logs instead of requesting nonexistent counters.
- The transport still explicitly omits multi-SPI and retains five-byte
  approvals. v7 does not solve the host/transport quad gap.

## Posted spi-mem quad transport RFC

**Verified posting:** Jizhou Tong's 2026-10-10
[Romulus13 RFC 0/6](https://lists.openwall.net/linux-kernel/2026/10/10/957)
(Message-ID `010601a126bfbf4d-27ca6d8c-0c4e-4191-a92d-6dc7d3fbac60-000000@ap-northeast-1.amazonses.com`)
is on SPI-HID **v5 plus two error-handling fixes**. Its
[patch 2/6](https://lkml.iu.edu/2610.1/05652.html) encodes quad operations
through spi-mem: one-lane opcode, three-byte quad address, four quad dummy
bytes/eight clocks, and quad report data. Patch 1/6 isolates DMA buffers;
remaining patches include Romulus fragmentation, binding and reset behavior.

The cover explicitly says its test kernel retains **downstream GENI/GPI
QSPI and Romulus DT dependencies outside this HID series**. Therefore:

- This is posted **transport** quad support for a Surface Laptop 7
  touchpad, not a posted `spi-geni-qcom` quad/dual controller series.
- It does not invalidate the session-2 negative result for an upstream GENI
  controller backport. No generic GENI multi-lane SoC support list was found.
- It supplies a better transport design lead than reinterpreting full-duplex
  transfers or inventing a HID-specific host opcode heuristic. Prefer its
  protocol/DMA changes when forward-porting onto v7.
- Its Romulus opcodes/timing/iptsd heatmap rebind behavior and fragmentation
  are not evidence for this unit. Do not transplant them into MSHW0484.
- The author's SL7 working/build/test reports are external reports; they
  are not local SP11 acceptance or local build results.

This RFC has not been copied/applied as an integration patch. Sources should
pin it and its exact base/fixes. Kernel should assess its minimal transport
pieces with the host/GPI design and current SE-state capture. Linux 7.3's
`spi_mem_exec_op()` already supports mem_ops and a regular-message fallback
(`sources/linux/drivers/spi/spi-mem.c:392–505`); the fallback still requires
actual host multi-lane support. Merely enabling SPI_MEM cannot repair GENI.
