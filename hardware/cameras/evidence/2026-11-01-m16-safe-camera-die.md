# M16 safe boot: camera probe and the missing DIE

## Evidence and limits

- **Verified (operator transcription, 2026-11-01):** On the real Surface Pro 11, M16 private ISO, kernel `7.3.0-65.sp11.14.fc44`, a DIE and taint `D` preceded 46.66 s; the available call-trace tail contained `worker_thread`, `kthread`, `ret_from_fork`. At 47.72 s the operator transcribed `drivers/gpu/drm/drm_vblank.c:1329`, `msm_dpu ae01000.display-controller: [drm] vblank wait timed out on crtc 0`, and `drm_crtc_wait_one_vblank <- drm_client_modeset_wait_for_vblank <- drm_fb_helper_fb_dirty <- drm_fb_helper_damage_work`. The WARN's `Modules linked in` included `i2c_qcom_cci(+)`, `camcc_x1e80100`, `msm`, and `panel_edp`; the operator reports repeated WARNs. These are the exact fragments supplied, not a continuous verbatim log. The DIE header, fault address, faulting PC, full trace and preceding messages are unavailable. **Test:** recover persistent full kernel log or record the whole screen from boot, including the first fault.
- **Verified (operator report):** M15.1 device logs lacked the vblank warning, `i2c_qcom_cci` load and oops; cameras entered m15-safe in M16, kernel commit `1d92d8a`. **Test:** compare the two complete boot logs and exact DTBs/configuration, if preserved.
- **Inference:** `(+)` indicates the module was loading at the later WARN, not proof of where the earlier DIE occurred; the DRM stack identifies the later warning's execution path, not its initiating cause. A GPU/display issue or a consequence of an earlier fault remain possible. **Test:** full first oops plus controlled boots described below.

## A concrete CCI defect

- **Verified:** `kernel/dtb-variants/x1p64100-microsoft-denali-m15-safe.dts` enables `cci1` and `cci1_i2c1` but not `cci1_i2c0`; `hardware/cameras/patches/0003-arm64-dts-qcom-hamoa-describe-disabled-camera-blocks.patch:64` defines master 0 disabled and master 1 with `reg = <1>`. Both CCI nodes use fallback `qcom,msm8996-cci` (same patch lines 34–90), matched to `cci_v2_data` by `sources/linux/drivers/i2c/busses/i2c-qcom-cci.c:807`; no camera patch 0001–0020 modifies that driver. The CCI code in `out/linux/kernel-m15` and `out/linux/integration` has the same completion initialization/reset pattern; the running binary itself was not inspected. **Test:** extract and compare the exact booted DTB and RPM source/config against these inputs.
- **Verified:** `cci_probe()` initializes `master->irq_complete` only in the loop over available child nodes (`sources/linux/drivers/i2c/busses/i2c-qcom-cci.c:523`, `:562`), then enables clocks, requests the IRQ and calls `cci_reset()` before registering I2C adapters (`:573–619`). `cci_reset()` unconditionally reinitializes and waits on master 0 (`:271–282`); its reset IRQ also completes master 0 (`:143–147`). `reinit_completion()` resets only `done`; `init_completion()` also initializes the wait queue (`sources/linux/include/linux/completion.h:84–100`). The completion wait path manipulates that queue (`sources/linux/kernel/sched/completion.c:95–122`).
- **Inference:** With master 0 disabled on CCI1, the probe uses an uninitialized wait queue. If reset reaches the wait or interrupt path, invalid queue operations can fault even without a sensor, firmware blob, CAMSS binding, or streaming. The exact failing instruction depends on timing and configuration. The same defect applies to any CCI with only master 1 enabled, including the rear-only M13 variant (`kernel/dtb-variants/x1p64100-microsoft-denali-m13-cam.dts`). **Test:** on a controlled diagnostic kernel, unit-test initialization for no enabled child, only master 1, only master 0 and both; then compare a captured device oops with a fixed driver in an otherwise identical diagnostic boot. Do not deliberately reproduce an unpatched crash on the recovery boot.
- **Hypothesis:** This defect produced the observed earlier DIE. **Test:** obtain its faulting PC and full trace showing `cci_reset`/completion or its reset IRQ, and confirm disappearance with the completion fix in otherwise identical repeated boots. If the trace identifies an unrelated fault, reject attribution. A clean gated boot alone supports camera-stack involvement but does not isolate CCI or prove causality.
- **Inference / proposed driver correction:** Initialize all `cci->master[i].irq_complete` immediately after allocation and before `devm_request_irq`, outside the available-child loop; remove the later per-child initialization. This covers reset and either IRQ master, including no available child, without registering an unwanted adapter or reinitializing an active completion. Review and test as a separate kernel change; it does not establish the rest of the camera stack is safe. **Test:** regression tests for all child combinations, timeout and reset-IRQ races, then controlled hardware boot.

## Node-by-node probe review

- **Verified:** CCI0 at `0x0ac15000` and CCI1 at `0x0ac16000` each declare three camera clocks, a Titan top power domain and IRQ; the driver obtains all declared clocks, maps resource 0, reads the version, writes reset and awaits an IRQ (`patches/0003:34–90`; `sources/linux/drivers/i2c/busses/i2c-qcom-cci.c:567–603`). CCI0 masters 0 and 1 are both enabled and initialized in this variant; CCI1 master 1 alone exposes the defect above. Missing clocks or IRQ normally return errors, while incorrect but mappable MMIO/clock/power settings could fault or hang during hardware access. **TBD:** physical addresses, GDSC behavior, IRQs and clocks on this device are not independently proven by these DT declarations. **Test:** compare an authoritative hardware description and non-invasive resource/clock logs, then test each controller in isolation with the completion fixed.
- **Verified:** CCI0 buses 0 and 1 use `reg` 0/1 and 400 kHz; CCI1 bus 1 uses `reg` 1 and 400 kHz. The driver bounds-checks master index and chooses fast-mode timings for 400 kHz (`i2c-qcom-cci.c:523–562`; `patches/0014:67–71`, `0018:113–122`, `0019:99–109`). CCI0 pinctrl selects GPIO101–104; CCI1 uses GPIO235/236; sensor reset and MCLK pinctrl are separate (`patches/0005`, `0018:124–135,172–190`, `0019:121–128,160–178`). **TBD:** correct physical muxing, contention and board wiring at runtime. **Test:** inspect pinctrl ownership and errors on an isolated fixed diagnostic boot; do not scan I2C buses.
- **Verified:** `camss` is the `isp@acb7000` node, not an additional separate enabled ISP node; patch 0003 lines 94–252 declares named register regions, clocks, IRQs, four interconnects and three power domains. The X1E80100 resource tables name the corresponding CSIPHY, CSID, TPG, VFE and interconnect resources (`sources/linux/drivers/media/platform/qcom/camss/camss.c:4190–4532,5729–5739`); probe gets interconnects and domains, initializes subdevices and parses endpoints (`:5355–5445`); patches 0007–0010 change PHY handling. This is a static name/flow review, not a physical address validation or proof that every resource value is correct. Named-resource failures generally return errors; bad hardware mappings or power/clock sequencing remain plausible faults. **TBD:** CSIPHY rails, power-domain sequencing, MMIO/IRQ routing, endpoint and interconnect behavior on this machine. **Test:** with CCI disabled and complete early logs, boot a CAMSS-only diagnostic after independent resource review, then examine probe and power-domain errors.
- **Verified:** The IR node has only `compatible = "st,vd55g0"` (`patches/0019:117–149`); `vd55g1` matches only `st,vd55g1`, `st,vd55g4`, `st,vd65g4` (`sources/linux/drivers/media/i2c/vd55g1.c:2079–2085`). Patch 0017 changes the binding schema, not that match table. Thus ordinary OF matching does not bind this IR node to `vd55g1`; it describes clock, reset, three supplies and an endpoint, but the sensor driver does not consume them. IR illuminator and flash are not described. **Test:** inspect I2C device modalias and driver symlink, without force-binding or streaming.
- **Verified:** Rear OV13858 at CCI0 master 1 declares MCLK1, reset GPIO110, `dovdd`, `dvdd`, `avdd`, endpoint and privacy LED (`patches/0005:103–140`, `0014:75–100`). Patch 0002 acquires these supplies and reset and powers on before chip identification; failures return through probe paths. Patch 0013 requests `ov13858-mshw0491.bin` before power-on and falls back to built-in modes if missing or invalid (`patches/0013:493–548`). CCI controller reset precedes adapter creation, so a fault in that controller's reset precedes this sensor's probe. **TBD:** actual regulator suppliers, voltage/power order, pin state and blob bytes on M16. **Test:** inspect deferred-probe and regulator/clock/pinctrl logs with fixed CCI and independently verified privacy LED; no streaming until LED wiring is verified.
- **Verified:** Front IMX681 at CCI1 master 1 declares MCLK4, reset GPIO237, `dovdd` and `avdd`, endpoint and privacy LED (`patches/0018:118–159`); patch 0016 requests `imx681-mshw0490.bin` and parses it before clock/supplies/reset and power-on, and returns an error if firmware is absent (`patches/0016:894–970`). A missing blob does not protect CCI1's earlier controller reset. **TBD:** M16 blob presence/validity, actual rail wiring, clock, pinctrl and chip response. **Test:** inspect sanitized firmware request/probe logs and resource ownership after the controller fix, without streaming.
- **Verified:** Enabled `camera-leds` instantiates two GPIO LEDs; patch 0014 adds GPIO105 while patch 0004 defines the other indicator. Patch 0020 acquires a shared per-LED reference during sensor subdevice registration and changes streaming counts on stream transitions (`patches/0020:142–250, 310–440`). Allocation failures propagate; the reference registry uses a mutex. Its KUnit suite was compiled but not run (dossier log). **Inference:** LED registration/refcount can be reached later during sensor probe, but the CCI reset bug occurs before adapters and sensor registration; no evidence links patch 0020 to this DIE. **TBD:** actual LED pin mapping and runtime races. **Test:** execute the KUnit suite and perform independently cross-checked LED observation during a controlled, authorized camera stream; never enable the IR illuminator or flash.

## Mitigation and discrimination

- **Inference:** The conservative recovery measure is the camera gate in `hardware/cameras/m16-safe-camera-gate.diff`: disable both CCI parents and enabled buses, CAMSS and camera LEDs in m15-safe. Sensor children remain described but inaccessible behind disabled controllers. Camera rails enabled by the variant remain unchanged because they may be shared. Since `m15.dts` includes m15-safe, it also inherits this gate; `m15-spectra.dts` has its own downstream stack and explicitly disables upstream CCI/CAMSS. Independently built historical/debug camera variants are not fixed by this change and may expose the same bug. **Test:** kernel owner validates the compiled effective DTB, including ancestry and aliases; compare repeated gated vs original boots with otherwise identical images and full early logs.
- **TBD:** The relative roles of the CCI defect and the later vblank timeout are unknown. **Test:** capture full boot logs, first compare gated recovery boots, then only after the CCI fix and independent privacy-wiring review run single-variable diagnostic boots. A persistent DRM WARN with cameras gated points to a separate display path; disappearance does not alone prove the CCI defect caused it.
